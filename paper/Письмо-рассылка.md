## Вариант 1 — тем, на кого мы ссылаемся

**Subject:** Draft: what is worth inheriting between buildings — and a measurement platform for it

Dear Professor [Name],

Transfer of operating experience between technical objects is now a large question — buildings, machines, water networks all face it. I started with buildings because they are the best documented.

Most work asks how to transfer. I wanted to ask what: given an object with a history, which part of it is worth keeping so another object can use it. That question comes before method, and I could not find it measured anywhere, so I built a stand to measure it.

The draft is attached. Three domains, one protocol: an object lives once, no replay, losses accumulate. Partly on a synthetic population, partly on measured data — 40 real buildings from BDG2 and six benchmark water networks.

Some results are negative and I report them as they came. Similarity of bodies predicts nothing. A single predecessor is usually harmful. What transfers is where to watch, not what to set. I also include three defects I found in my own instrument after the experiments were done, and what each one changed.

I am an independent researcher and have no group to argue with. That is why I am writing to you.

If you have twenty minutes, I would value your view on one thing above all: whether the protocol measures what I claim it measures, or whether I have fooled myself somewhere.

Evgenii Bogomazov-Pusser

Прошу двух вещей.

Первое — посмотрите глазами человека, который здания видел вживую, и скажите, где я написал ерунду. Физика у меня упрощённая, и я это знаю, но мне важно, где именно она упрощена до неправды.

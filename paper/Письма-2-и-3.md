Dear Professor Capozzoli,

Your review with Pinto, Wang, Roy and Hong on transfer learning for smart buildings is the paper I kept returning to. Two things in it stayed with me: that negative transfer is recognised but its causes are not settled, and that the selection of source buildings is still open.

I came at both from an odd direction, and I would like to know whether the direction is wrong.

Instead of asking which source building to choose, I asked what to transfer at all. The draft is attached. One protocol across three domains — a synthetic population, 40 measured buildings from BDG2, and six benchmark water networks — where an object lives once, without replay, and pays its losses as they occur.

Two results touch your review directly.

On source selection: I could not find any measure of similarity that predicts whether transfer helps. Correlation of gain with difference in floor area is −0.001, with year of construction −0.06. Buildings of the same declared use lose 82 % of accuracy against 86 % for a different use. In the synthetic domain an apparent correlation of +0.21 with physical distance turned out to be an artefact of my own cost function and fell to +0.05 when I repaired it.

On negative transfer: most of the harm disappeared when I changed not the method but the units. The same records re-expressed in quantities defined relative to the receiving building raised the share of buildings helped from 81–84 % to 99 %. If that holds outside my stand, negative transfer is partly a representation problem rather than a similarity problem.

I am an independent researcher and have no group to test this against. If you have twenty minutes, I would value your view on whether the protocol measures what I claim, or whether I have fooled myself somewhere.

With respect,

Dear Professor Miller,

I used BDG2 for the measured part of the attached draft, and I am writing partly to tell you what it gave me and partly to ask about the one thing it cannot give.

The experiment: site Rat, 40 buildings with at least 95 % coverage, load shape predicted from hour, day of week, month, weekend and outdoor temperature. 1 560 ordered pairs of donor and receiver. A newcomer is a building with only the first 14 days of 2016.

A donor's whole model applied to another building loses a median of 85 % of accuracy and is worse than the receiver's own model in 93 % of pairs. A model trained on all other buildings and then refined on the newcomer's 14 days is the only condition that helps a majority — 62 %. For the remaining 38 % it stays harmful, and I found no metadata property that separates them in advance.

The same ordering appeared in a synthetic population and in six water networks, which is the main reason I trust it at all.

Now the thing BDG2 cannot answer, and it is the largest gap in the paper. The dataset has meter readings and nothing else — no setpoints, no schedules, no log of operator intervention. So what I could transfer between buildings was a predictive model, never a policy. The question I actually want to ask is what an operator's accumulated decisions are worth to a building that has none.

Do you know of an open dataset that carries setpoints and a record of interventions alongside consumption? I know of instrumented reference buildings and of donated thermostat records, but nothing at the scale and quality of BDG2.

And if there is anything in the paper's use of your data that you would want corrected, I would rather hear it now than after it is posted.

With thanks for the dataset, which is the reason the measured part of this work exists at all,

Evgenii Bogomazov-Pusser
Independent researcher
evgeniipusser88@gmail.com

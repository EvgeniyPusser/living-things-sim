## Письмо учёному

**Subject:** Cloud DNA: a shared fund of operating experience for technical objects

Dear [Name],

Technical objects that work for decades — factories, machines, buildings — each accumulate a history that belongs only to them. When the object is rebuilt or replaced, that history is lost, and the next one starts from generic settings. This happens everywhere and almost nobody treats it as a question.

I work on all three as an engineer, not from data alone. My starting point is that such objects are not dead infrastructure and not simply instruments. They already have what in living systems is called self-regulation: they hold conditions, they recover after failure, they are optimised over years. If that is so, then the other functions of the living can be asked about seriously — restoration, adaptation, retention, inheritance — and asked precisely rather than by analogy.

The idea I am working towards is **Cloud DNA**: a common fund of operating experience that technical objects draw on, because no single object lives long enough to learn what it needs. A building meets a hard frost once in ten years. A network needs dozens of failures before it knows where to watch. The individual cannot pay that price; a population can.

The difficulty is not storage. It is that two objects write their experience in different terms, like two combs with different teeth missing. Without a common denominator there is nothing to transfer.

I am writing a series towards this. The first part is terminology. The attached paper is the second: one function — inheritance — put on a test bench, with buildings and water networks as material, not as subject. The results are mostly negative and I report them as they came. The one that matters for the idea: a building has no genotype and no reversible mapping from it, so inheritance for such objects cannot be genetic at all. It can only be a fund. Measured: the quality a predecessor achieved does not pass to its successors, while a fund over many helps 97–98 %.

The code is open and runs on an ordinary laptop. I write it with AI assistance and say so in the paper — the questions, the protocol and the interpretation are mine, and I would rather state that plainly than have it assumed.

I am an independent researcher and have no group to argue with. I would like to know whether this line is of interest to you, and whether it is worth a conversation.

With respect,

Evgenii Bogomazov-Pusser
